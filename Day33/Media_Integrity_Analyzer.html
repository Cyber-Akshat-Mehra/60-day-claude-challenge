🔍 Challenge 1 · Headline Detective

Can headlines lie without lying?

The concept: Publishers often write headlines designed to trigger clicks, not to accurately summarize the story. This is called clickbait. Words like "shocking," "you won't believe," or vague teasers ("this one habit") are designed to create a curiosity gap — a question your brain feels compelled to answer.

Why it matters: Clickbait headlines shape what we believe is true before we've even read the article — sometimes we never do. Misleading headlines spread faster than corrections.

💓 Challenge 2 · Emotion Detector

Feelings are a lever — someone is pulling it

The concept: Content creators and advertisers often craft posts to trigger a specific emotion — fear, outrage, envy, urgency — because emotional content spreads faster and drives more engagement than neutral content.

Why it matters: When we're emotionally activated, we think less critically and share more impulsively. Recognizing the emotional "hook" is the first step to resisting it.

Three habits to practice from now on

1

Pause before reacting. If a headline or post makes you feel something strongly, that's your cue to slow down before sharing.

2

Read past the headline. Check whether the body actually supports the claim made in the title.

3

Check the source and intent. Ask who benefits from you feeling this way, and whether the source has a track record of accuracy.
